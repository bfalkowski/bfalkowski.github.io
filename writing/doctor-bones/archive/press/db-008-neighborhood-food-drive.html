Neighbors turn out for annual food drive

A modest neighborhood effort has grown into a reliable source of donations for a local pantry, organizers say.
By Elaine Mercer  |  September 13, 2008

DAYTON — Folding tables filled the fellowship hall Saturday morning as volunteers sorted canned vegetables, pasta, cereal and boxes of household staples collected during the Westwood neighborhood's annual food drive.

The event began as a small collection among a few nearby households. Organizers say it has gradually become a familiar September project, with residents dropping off bags throughout the morning and volunteers making deliveries to a community pantry later in the day.

“People know it is coming, and they set a few things aside,” said Dale Hubbard, one of the volunteer organizers. “It is not complicated. You ask your neighbors to help, and most people find a way.”

Hubbard, who works as an independent insurance agent in the Dayton area, said the drive depends on a small group that handles the practical details: arranging collection points, sorting donations and coordinating transportation. He said his wife, Marjorie, helped circulate the reminder notice to nearby households.

Several volunteers said the event's strength is its predictability. Residents who cannot attend on the collection day leave donations with a neighbor, while others contribute money for pantry staples that are difficult to collect in large quantities.

Organizers estimated that this year's collection filled more than two dozen boxes. The final count was expected after volunteers completed the afternoon delivery.
