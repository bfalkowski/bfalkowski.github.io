For these crossword regulars, the clues are only half the fun

A weekly puzzle group has turned a quiet Thursday gathering into a friendly contest of memory, patience and the occasional argument over a three-letter answer.
By Elaine Porter   |   Ledger Community Correspondent

The first rule of the Thursday crossword table is that nobody is allowed to say an answer too loudly. The second is that nobody is required to follow the first rule.

By 10 a.m., the long table at the fictional Westwood Neighborhood Center is covered with coffee cups, sharpened pencils and folded newspapers. A dozen regulars work through the week's puzzles, trading theories and occasionally defending an answer with the determination of a trial lawyer.

“You can know a lot of things and still stare at a four-letter word for twenty minutes,” said longtime participant Helen Price, laughing as the group debated a clue about an old movie star.

The gathering began as an informal way for neighbors to spend a morning together. It now draws a steady group from across west Dayton. Some come for the competition; others say the best part is the conversation that fills the spaces between clues.

“The trick is not to rush it. Let the crossing words tell you what you missed.”

Among the regulars is Marjorie Hubbard, whose friends say she has an unusual knack for spotting a theme before the rest of the table has finished the top half of the puzzle. She is especially good with wordplay and old-fashioned expressions, though she insists that the group gives her too much credit.

“I just do them every morning,” Hubbard said. “After a while you start to recognize the setter's habits. And if you don't know something, somebody at this table usually does.”

The group keeps an informal tally during its occasional timed puzzle days, but nobody takes the standings too seriously. The winner gets first choice of the doughnuts the following week. The losers get to argue about the answers.

For the regulars, that seems to be the point. The puzzle gives everyone a reason to show up, and the friendships give them a reason to stay after the last square is filled.

The Thursday crossword group meets weekly at the Westwood Neighborhood Center. Marjorie Hubbard is among the regular participants. (Photo not available in the online edition.)
