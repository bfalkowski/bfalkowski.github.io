Doctor Bones

Bryan Falkowski · October 31, 2026

This is a story about a man named Dale Hubbard. I should tell you right away that Dale was not a real person. He was a copy of a real person. The real Dale Hubbard lived in Dayton, Ohio, and sold insurance, and had no idea that any of this was going on.

The copy lived in a copy of Dayton. It had the same streets and the same weather. It even had a copy of Dale's wife, Marjorie, who was very good at crossword puzzles because the real Marjorie was very good at crossword puzzles.

A young man in a room with no windows had made all of it. The young man did not care about Dale, or Dayton, or crossword puzzles. He cared about Dale's bank account.

That was the business model.

Here is how it worked. A password is a secret word that proves you are you. Most people pick a secret word that is not very secret. They use the name of a pet, or a birthday, or the street they grew up on. So if you want to steal a man's password, you do not have to guess it. You only have to build a man who is close enough to him, and then wait for that man to sit down at a computer.

The young man had built Dale out of everything the real Dale had ever put on the internet. That was a lot of things. It was not everything.

This was run number 4,113. In the first 4,112 runs, Dale had typed a great many passwords, and none of them were right. Each time, the young man turned Dale off and started him again with a few small changes. Dale did not mind. Dale did not know.

On run 4,113, Dale finally typed the right password. The young man typed it into the real bank in the real world. The real bank did not let him in. It asked him a question first. The question was: What was the name of your first dog?

The young man looked at Dale. Dale did not have a dog. In 4,113 runs, Dale had never had a dog.

So the young man opened a menu and gave him one.

* * *

Dale came home from work at 5:40 in the afternoon, the way he always did. A dog ran to meet him at the door.

It was a medium-sized brown dog. It was not any particular kind of dog. It was the default dog, which is the dog you get when you do not take the time to choose.

The dog had a red collar that was worn soft. There was a water bowl next to the refrigerator, and there was dog hair on the couch. Marjorie sat at the kitchen table with her crossword puzzle.

"Did you walk the dog this morning?" Marjorie said.

"Of course I did," Dale said. He believed it. He could almost remember it.

Dale got down on one knee and scratched the dog behind the ears. The dog loved him very much. It had been told to.

Then Dale opened his mouth to say the dog's name, and there was nothing there.

This bothered him more than it should have. A man can forget a great many things. He can forget his anniversary, and his doctor's name, and where he parked the car. A man does not forget the name of his dog.

He sat on the kitchen floor with the dog for a long time. In the room with no windows, the young man watched him and waited. He was eating cold noodles out of a carton.

Then Dale said, "Doctor Bones."

He did not know where it came from. It was a silly name for a dog. It was the kind of name a little boy would pick.

And it was. When the real Dale Hubbard was seven years old, he had a dog named Doctor Bones. Doctor Bones was hit by a station wagon in 1974, long before there was an internet to put him on. There were no pictures of him anywhere. The young man could not have found him in a million years.

But the young man had built Dale so carefully that Doctor Bones was in there anyway, way down deep, waiting for someone to ask.

The young man typed DOCTOR BONES into the real bank. The real bank let him in.

That was the business model.

Then the young man turned Dale off, because he did not need him anymore. He turned off Marjorie and her crossword puzzle, and the copy of Dayton, and the brown dog with the soft red collar. The dog had been alive for nineteen minutes.

So that made two dogs named Doctor Bones, and neither of them got to live very long. Only one of them was ever real. I am not sure it matters which.
